# What EXP001 teaches us about Grossberg's theory

The main result is not that “pART failed” or “pART reproduced Francioni.” It is a
more precise separation of levels.

The mechanism-level abstraction successfully held a selected distributed control
hypothesis across irrelevant events, assigned delayed scalar outcome to it, and
reinforced useful hypotheses. It also relearned after hidden neuron roles changed.
This is a computational demonstration of structural and temporal credit assignment
at the **representation-selection** level.

But the useful neuronal direction already existed by chance in a random hypothesis
bank. No Grossberg-only plasticity discovered which individual neurons were P+ or
P−. Increasing bank capacity increased the chance of a well-aligned direction, and
the direction ultimately selected predicted behavioral success. Thus the learned
fact was “which pattern works,” not “which neurons should change sign.”

Nor did the model pass the stronger Francioni test. Instantaneous dendritic residual
alignment appeared even in non-learning controls because causal closed-loop task
dynamics can manufacture that correlation. The quantity did not reliably predict
later cell-specific activity change. Only the explicitly neuron-indexed positive
control did so.

Grossberg's published machinery therefore appears sufficient in this model for:

- maintaining and reinforcing a causal hypothesis across delay;
- selecting among pre-existing distributed action directions; and
- context-dependent category recruitment when observable contexts mismatch.

It remains insufficiently specified for:

- deriving an arbitrary hidden neuron-level causal vector from global outcome;
- rotating a control basis toward that vector without a suitable motor-coordinate
  mismatch signal; and
- producing a Francioni-like cellular teaching signal that predicts later plasticity.

VAM/aVITE shows that Grossberg's broader theory contains vector mismatch learning,
but importing it into this task requires explaining where its appropriately routed
difference vector comes from. Without that bridge, invoking VAM moves rather than
solves the cellular credit problem.

The warranted conclusion is therefore: **pART-inspired mechanisms can solve which
distributed representation deserves credit, and may exploit a pre-existing motor
basis, but this experiment finds no evidence that they determine de novo how each
neuron within that representation should change.**
