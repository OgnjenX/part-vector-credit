# EXP005 conclusion

Status: **FINAL — SOURCE OUTCOME E; GENERIC DIAGNOSTIC POSITIVE**

Grossberg's audited published mechanisms do not specify the missing A2 cellular
operation. ART/pART assigns delayed outcome to a representation; ERG releases
exploration; outstars store a concurrent distributed pattern; SMART gates local
learning; VAM/cerebellar systems learn with structured error. The audited equations
do not join a neuron-local exploratory trace to delayed scalar consequence to learn
arbitrary intermingled RSC signs.

A separately labeled generic rule *can* do so. On 16 held-out mappings, local node
perturbations plus a scalar reward advantage learned near-chance topology to 0.987
role alignment at N=32, reached 0.964 after hidden remap, and correctly reversed
every changed cell. Shuffling scalar outcomes, removing exploration, or removing
the local delayed eligibility abolished the effect. The full local equation
reconstructed every update without hidden roles.

The result is therefore not “Grossberg explains Francioni.” It is:

> Scalar behavioral outcome can identify arbitrary neuron-resolved causal topology
> without an explicit neuron-wise error when each neuron contributes an independent
> local exploratory eligibility. That eligibility/outcome rule is an additional
> cellular-credit hypothesis not found in the audited Grossberg sources.

## Direct answers

1. **Does Grossberg publish the needed scalar-outcome topology rule?** No matching
   primary equation was found. The source outcome is E.
2. **What exact source-derived equations implement it?** None. Outstar, ERG, pART,
   SMART, VAM, and CogEM each supply adjacent functions but not the decisive local
   scalar-outcome conjunction.
3. **What is extrapolation?** A neuron-local perturbation trace, its retention until
   delayed outcome, and bidirectional multiplication by scalar advantage are new
   class-D assumptions.
4. **What differs across neurons?** The retained perturbation eligibility `e_i`.
   Global `(R-b)` is identical; `e_i` makes `Delta A_i` cell-specific.
5. **Can random hidden roles be learned from uninformative topology?** Yes for the
   generic comparator, with held-out alignment 0.997–0.972 across N after acquisition.
6. **Does final alignment exceed initialization?** Decisively; initial intervals all
   include zero, final intervals are near one.
7. **Does it exceed every exploratory sample?** Yes during acquisition at every N;
   after remap at N=32/64, but not N=8/16.
8. **Can legal variables reconstruct learning?** Yes, to numerical precision, using
   local eligibility, assigned scalar outcome, baseline, bounds, and centering.
9. **Does outcome shuffling abolish learning?** It abolishes the large causal effect;
   N=32 post-remap alignment falls from 0.964 to 0.229.
10. **Does removing exploration abolish learning?** Yes; weights remain at their
    initial values.
11. **Can neuron roles reverse after remap?** Yes; changed-cell accuracy is 1.0 up to
    N=32 and 0.975 at N=64.
12. **Does ART beat a contextual bandit?** Not tested in EXP005 because the source
    gate prohibited inventing a Grossberg primary rule. EXP004 already found no ART
    advantage in its fixed-repertoire task.
13. **What could ART still contribute?** Representation/context ownership and
    stability-plasticity. The generic local rule, not ART, solves cellular topology
    in this diagnostic.
14. **Does generic node perturbation succeed where the Grossberg candidate fails?**
    The generic rule succeeds; no legitimate Grossberg candidate existed to run.
15. **Does generic success require privileged neuron-wise information?** No. Only
    the isolated oracle receives hidden roles.
16. **How does it scale?** Final topology remains at least 0.95 at N=64 under a
    transparent learning-gain correction for the BCI's known `1/N` normalization.
    The local variation/outcome signal becomes noisier with N, falling from 0.982
    to 0.499 in role alignment, and threshold acquisition slows from about 95 to
    133 episodes.
17. **Implication for A2?** A2 is not supported as an already published Grossberg
    mechanism. It becomes a concrete hybrid hypothesis: pART-like ownership plus a
    new local perturbation/eligibility credit rule.
18. **Strongest interpretation?** **Generic three-factor cellular credit**—not
    repertoire selection, not source-derived Grossberg credit, and not proof that
    explicit neuron-wise teaching is biologically required.
19. **Best biological discriminator?** Measure whether spontaneous or imposed
    cell-specific pre-outcome RSC deviations leave local traces whose product with a
    later scalar reinforcement signal predicts subsequent signed plasticity. A
    perturbation account predicts this interaction and its loss when trace/reward
    timing is decorrelated; an explicit vector-error account need not depend on
    exploratory covariance.

## Claim boundary

Success establishes computational sufficiency of a derivative-free, scalar-reward
three-factor rule in this abstract BCI. It does not establish that RSC uses node
perturbation, that Francioni's dendritic signal is this eligibility, or that pART
already contains it. Failure of the audited source bridge is not failure of ART,
pART, or SMART globally. What remains unresolved is the biological identity of the
cell-local eligibility, where it is stored, how delayed outcome reaches it, and
whether Francioni's apical/NDNF-dependent signal implements a perturbation trace, a
vectorized instruction, selective gating, or another mechanism.
